1. Blood Venipuncture: Drawing blood into color-coded tubes (e.g., EDTA, serum separator) for plasma or serum analysis.
1. Finger Prick Micro-sampling: Using devices like Tasso or BD Microtainer for less invasive, patient-centric blood collection.
1. Bone Marrow Aspiration: Sampling the liquid component of bone marrow for hematologic assessment.
1. Bone Marrow Biopsy: Removing a small amount of solid bone marrow tissue for pathological examination.
1. Urine Collection (Clean-Catch): Collecting random urine in a leak-proof container for general urinalysis.
1. Timed Urine Collection: Discarding the first void and collecting all subsequent urine for a set duration for quantitative tests.
1. Cerebrospinal Fluid (CSF) Tap: Collecting CSF via lumbar puncture, often mixed with serum for cell counts.
1. Fine Needle Aspiration (FNA): Using a needle to extract cells from masses, with rinses placed in CytoLyt or sterile containers.
1. Anal PapTest: Using a swab/brush rinsed in PreservCyt solution for anal cytology.
1. Cervical Cytology (SurePath): Using a spatula/brush rotated in the cervix and placed directly into a SurePath vial.
1. Cervical Cytology (ThinPrep): Rotating a broom/brush and rinsing it into a ThinPrep vial to dislodge cells.
1. Broncho-Alveolar Lavage (BAL): Collecting lung fluid in an empty sterile container, requiring immediate lab submission.
1. Sputum Expectoration: Collecting early morning deep-cough sputum in a sterile container for culture or smear.
1. Surgical Tissue Biopsy: Placing excised tissue in 10% formalin at a 10:1 ratio to preserve structure.
1. Lymphoma/Culture Tissue: Sending fresh tissue in sterile saline-dampened gauze without fixative.
1. Gout Crystal Analysis: Sending fresh tissue in 100% alcohol or fresh without fixative.
1. Body Fluid Cytology: Collecting pleural, ascitic, or pericardial fluids in sterile containers, often with Cytolyt solution.
1. Endoscopic Brushings: Placing brush samples into 15ml CytoLyt solution for cellular analysis.
1. Acid Fast Sputum/Tissue Smear: Collecting 5–10 mL of sputum or 2 grams of unfixed tissue in sterile saline for AFB testing.
1. Blood Culture: Drawing 20 mL of blood into aerobic and anaerobic bottles to detect bacteremia
